# Modern firearm suppressor designs, market and technology (state as of 2026)

Scope note: descriptive/taxonomic reference for game design. No machining dimensions or build guidance collected. Published catalogue specs (overall length, weight, diameter, material, calibre) only. Many spec figures below come from retailer/dealer listings surfaced by search rather than from manufacturer pages read directly; where sources disagreed, both figures are given. Items I could not source are in the Gaps lists rather than stated as fact.

Reliability key used below: **[primary]** = manufacturer, government or patent; **[trade]** = established trade press / test lab (TFB, PEW Science, Gun Digest, American Rifleman, Military Times); **[retail]** = dealer listing; **[wiki]** = Wikipedia or mirror.

## 1. Brief history: Maxim to the modern era

### Takeaway
The lineage runs: Maxim's commercial "silencer" (patent 1909) -> WWII clandestine integrals (HDM, Welrod, De Lisle) -> Cold War special-purpose systems built around subsonic ammunition (MP5SD, PBS-1, AS Val/VSS) and captive-piston "silent cartridges" (PSS/SP-4) -> modern durable all-metal baffle cans with no wear parts, now issued as general infantry equipment. The big technical shift was from consumable wipes/packing (a handful of shots) to all-metal baffles (tens of thousands of rounds).

### Cited Findings
- Pre-Maxim patents: Jakob Stahel (Switzerland) patented a silencer in 1892 intended for slaughtering cattle; C.A. Aeppli patented another in 1894. [wiki] — [Wikipedia: Silencer (firearms)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms))
- Hiram Percy Maxim is usually credited with the first commercially successful silencer (c. 1902), sold as the "Maxim Silencer"; patent granted 30 March 1909. Theodore Roosevelt was a known customer. [wiki] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms))
- Date conflict: sources variously give 1902 (invention/filing), 1908 (first sales) and 1909 (patent grant). Use "patented 1909" as the safe statement. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms)); [SilencerCo: evolution of suppressor technology](https://silencerco.com/blog/evolution-of-suppressor-technology-maxim-to-current); [Sandboxx](https://www.sandboxx.us/news/silent-but-violent-the-history-of-suppressors)
- The Maxim Model 1910 is described as the most widely distributed silencer in the US, and the Model 1912 as the first mass-marketed silencer designed specifically for military use (attribution among the history articles returned by search is not exact; treat as secondary). — [Sandboxx](https://www.sandboxx.us/news/silent-but-violent-the-history-of-suppressors); [Coffee or Die](https://coffeeordie.com/suppressors-history/)
- High Standard HDM: .22 LR pistol with integral suppressor, favoured by the OSS in WWII. OSS director William Donovan demonstrated it to President Roosevelt in the Oval Office (firing into a sandbag). [wiki] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms)); [Sandboxx](https://www.sandboxx.us/news/silent-but-violent-the-history-of-suppressors)
- Welrod: British single-shot-per-cycle, bolt-action pistol with integral silencer, built for covert work; roughly 2,800 made in WWII; used by SOE and OSS in occupied Europe. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms)); [Sandboxx](https://www.sandboxx.us/news/silent-but-violent-the-history-of-suppressors)
- De Lisle carbine: Lee-Enfield action converted to .45 ACP with an integral suppressor, designed by William De Lisle, used in small numbers by British special forces; measured 85.5 dB in official firing tests. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms)); [Sandboxx](https://www.sandboxx.us/news/silent-but-violent-the-history-of-suppressors)
- .22 LR and .45 ACP are regarded as the easiest cartridges to suppress, with technology dating to the 1940s. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms))
- Wipes: flexible dividers the bullet physically passes through; common through the Vietnam era; typically last about five shots and degrade accuracy, so most modern suppressors do not use them. Packing: steel wool degrades within about ten shots; metal mesh lasts hundreds to thousands. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms))
- Baffles: rigid dividers forming a series of expansion chambers; bore hole at least ~1 mm larger than bullet diameter (i.e. the bullet never touches); most suppressors have roughly 4 to 15 chambers. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms))
- H&K MP5SD: integrally suppressed SMG; ports just ahead of the chamber bleed gas so standard 115/124 gr supersonic 9 mm leaves the barrel subsonic. Described as "perhaps the most famous" integrally suppressed weapon. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms)); [Breach Bang Clear: integrally suppressed firearms through the years](https://www.breachbangclear.com/?p=128292)
- Soviet PBS-1: muzzle suppressor for the AKM, introduced in the 1960s, used mainly by Spetsnaz and KGB; two-chamber design using baffles plus a rubber wipe; intended for subsonic (7.62x39 "US") ammunition; the wipe needs replacement after roughly 20-25 rounds; cited reduction about 15 dB. The design also deliberately raises back pressure so the AK action cycles with subsonic ammunition. — [Wikipedia: PBS-1 silencer](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/PBS-1_silencer); [Shooting Illustrated: Dead Air Wolverine PBS-1](https://www.shootingillustrated.com/articles/2020/2/20/range-review-dead-air-wolverine-pbs-1)
- Modern homage: Dead Air Wolverine PBS-1 copies the Soviet look but uses a fully welded core of seven conical Stellite baffles instead of flat steel washers and a wipe. — [Shooting Illustrated](https://www.shootingillustrated.com/articles/2020/2/20/range-review-dead-air-wolverine-pbs-1); [Recoil](https://www.recoilweb.com/dead-air-wolverine-pbs-1-gemtech-arsenal-inc-ak-soviet-styling-american-execution-102311.html)
- AS Val and VSS Vintorez: integrally suppressed weapons in 9x39 mm, a heavy subsonic cartridge with armour-piercing loads that retains energy well at subsonic velocity. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms))
- Captive-piston ammunition (PSS pistol, SP-4 cartridge): a steel case holds a small charge behind an internal piston; on firing, the piston shoves the bullet out and then seals the case mouth, trapping the propellant gas inside the case. No suppressor is needed, so the gun is pocket-sized. The PSS is recoil-operated, has a two-part barrel, ejects cases conventionally, and accepts only SP-4. — [Wikipedia: PSS silent pistol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSS_silent_pistol); [TFB: The Soviet PSS pistol and captive-piston ammunition](https://www.thefirearmblog.com/blog/the-soviet-pss-pistol-and-the-rise-of-captive-piston-ammunition-guns-44826727); [Modern Firearms: PSS](https://modernfirearms.net/en/handguns/handguns-en/russia-semi-automatic-pistols/pss-besshumnyj-eng/)
- PSS sound figures conflict: 124.6 dB (comparable to a suppressed .22 pistol) in one source, 122 dB in another; the cartridge is listed as 7.62x41 mm or 7.62x42 mm depending on source. Related captive-piston designs: OTs-38 Stechkin revolver (about 110 dB cited for its rounds), earlier SP-3/PZAM cartridges with two-stage telescoping pistons, and the PSS-2. — [Wikipedia: Silencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms)); [Wikipedia: PSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSS_silent_pistol); [TFB](https://www.thefirearmblog.com/blog/the-soviet-pss-pistol-and-the-rise-of-captive-piston-ammunition-guns-44826727)
- Modern durability benchmark: the best rifle suppressors claim service lives above 30,000 rounds. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms))

### Inferences
- For a game taxonomy, history gives four distinct "tech tiers": (1) wipe/packed cans — very quiet for a few shots, then degrade, accuracy penalty; (2) integral ported-barrel weapons — quiet with any ammo but reduced muzzle velocity; (3) captive-piston ammunition — near-silent, tiny weapon, but special low-power ammo and low capacity; (4) modern all-metal baffle cans — no consumables, durable, but supersonic crack remains unless subsonic ammo is used.
- PBS-1 is a hybrid of tier 1 and tier 4 (baffles plus one wipe), which is a useful "Cold War" intermediate item with a consumable part.

### Gaps
- Suppressed Sten (Mk IIS / Mk VI): no source retrieved. Not covered in the Wikipedia silencer article section read. Needs a dedicated source (Imperial War Museums / Royal Armouries).
- PB pistol (6P9) and its two-part (fixed + detachable) suppressor: no source retrieved.
- Exact production numbers for De Lisle and HDM, and Welrod calibres/variants (Mk I 9 mm / Mk II .32 ACP): not retrieved.
- No source retrieved for the Vietnam-era US items (Mk 22 "Hush Puppy", MAC/Sionics) that bridge WWII and the modern commercial era.

## 2. Baffle design families (conceptual)

### Takeaway
All modern cans are a tube of expansion chambers separated by baffles; families differ in baffle shape and whether the stack is discrete parts or one piece. Cone-family baffles dominate centrefire rifle cans; K-baffles and monocores dominate rimfire and pistol cans; wipes survive only in ultra-compact pistol "K" cans. Sealed (welded/printed) is the norm for rifle; user-serviceable is the norm for rimfire and pistol because of lead and carbon fouling.

### Cited Findings
- Cone (conical) baffle: the long-established baseline shape. Rifle suppressors tend to use tall, steep cones; pistol suppressors use shorter, flatter ones. [primary, patent background text] — [US patent 8,100,224, "Suppressor with poly-conical baffles"](https://uspto.report/patent/grant/8100224)
- M-baffle: a cone baffle with the spacer/side wall integrated into the same part (so the cross-section resembles an M); patent background dates the style to about 1910. Wikipedia calls it an inverted cone and "the crudest" design. A consumer guide's claim that the M is a modified K-baffle conflicts with the patent lineage and should be disregarded. — [US patent 8,100,224](https://uspto.report/patent/grant/8100224); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms))
- K-baffle: a flat disc joined to a forward-facing cone, K-shaped in cross-section; described in patent background as the industry standard since the 1980s; creates cross-bore turbulence; main drawback is weight because it uses a lot of metal. — [US patent 8,100,224](https://uspto.report/patent/grant/8100224); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms))
- Other named types: Z-baffle (expensive to machine; dead-air pockets along the tube wall), Omega baffle (spaced cones with a scalloped mouth directing gas sideways), slant baffle, and monolithic core ("monocore", a single unitised core rather than a stack). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms)); [AFTE glossary](https://afte.org/?p=2338)
- Additively manufactured cans consolidate what used to be a welded stack into one monolithic build with no welds, threads or hand-stacked baffles. — [Nikon SLM Solutions white paper on metal AM suppressor manufacturing](https://nikon-slm-solutions.com/metal-am-suppressor-manufacturing-white-pape/)
- Examples of construction by class: Dead Air Mask HD (rimfire) uses a titanium tube with removable 17-4 stainless baffles; Dead Air Nomad-30 and Sandman (rifle) are fully welded; PWS BDE is printed titanium with modular removable baffles. — [American Rifleman: Mask HD review](https://www.americanrifleman.org/articles/2015/11/30/review-dead-air-armament-mask-hd-sound-suppressor/); [TFB: Nomad-30 first shots](https://www.thefirearmblog.com/blog/2018/10/11/dead-air-silencers-nomad/); [Gun Digest: 3D printed suppressors](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)

### Inferences
- Suggested game taxonomy (shape -> typical role): steep cone / stepped or radial cone -> centrefire rifle, sealed; K-baffle -> rimfire and pistol, take-apart; monocore -> rimfire, pistol, budget rifle, take-apart, easy to clean but often more first-round pop; wipe -> micro pistol cans, consumable; printed lattice/flow geometries -> modern rifle, sealed.
- Sealed vs serviceable is effectively a calibre question: dirty low-pressure rounds (rimfire, lead pistol) need cleaning; high-pressure rifle rounds largely self-clean and benefit from welded strength.

### Gaps
- "Radial"/"stepped cone" and "clipped" baffles (asymmetric notches to induce cross-jetting) are common industry terms but I retrieved no citable source describing them.
- First-round pop behaviour by baffle family and typical dB ranges by family: not sourced.
- Consumer guide pages returned by search were low quality (SEO spam); the guns.com 2026 "suppressor types" explainer was blocked (HTTP 403).

## 3. Conventional baffle stacks vs flow-through / low-back-pressure (LBP) designs

### Takeaway
Conventional cans trap gas, which is quiet at the muzzle but pushes gas back through the action (more gas in the shooter's face, higher cyclic rate, faster fouling and parts wear). Flow/LBP designs route gas forward through secondary paths to vent faster; they keep the host weapon running near unsuppressed behaviour but generally give up some suppression, especially with subsonic ammunition, and can flash more. By 2025-26 nearly every major maker has an LBP line and "hybrid" designs sit between the two poles.

### Cited Findings
- PEW Science's taxonomy (300 BLK research supplement): **Conventional** (early blast reflected rearward, later flow significantly restricted; e.g. Q Full Nelson, SureFire SOCOM300-SPS, Otter Creek Labs Hydrogen, SIG SRD762QD-Ti); **Flow-Through** (HUXWRX high-flow technology; e.g. HUXWRX/OSS HX-QD 762); **Forward Flux** (SIG's high-flow technology; e.g. SIG SLH300Ti); **Surge Bypass** (CAT's hybrid technology, described as "pressure agnostic" across supersonic and subsonic loads); other **hybrids** include CGS Hyperion / Hyperion K and the SureFire RC2 family. [trade] — [PEW Science SSS.6.124](https://pewscience.com/sound-signature-reviews-free/sss-6-124-research-supplement-flow-through-forward-flux-surge-bypass-conventional-300-blk-rifle-silencers)
- Stated trade-off: higher flow rate lowers back pressure but generally lowers suppression, particularly with subsonic ammunition; hybrids can perform across regimes. — [PEW Science SSS.6.124](https://pewscience.com/sound-signature-reviews-free/sss-6-124-research-supplement-flow-through-forward-flux-surge-bypass-conventional-300-blk-rifle-silencers)
- Flow-through suppressors tend to be slightly louder than baffle suppressors and can produce more muzzle flash. — [Pew Pew Tactical: flow-through suppressors](https://www.pewpewtactical.com/flow-through-suppressors/); [True Shot Academy](https://trueshotammo.com/academy/traditional-vs-flow-through-type-suppressors)
- SureFire SOCOM556-RC3: SureFire's first low-back-pressure can; identifiable by a flared, welded flow-through front cap; SureFire claims 60% less back pressure than the RC2; kept the RC2's envelope so existing weapon setups need no changes; SureFire says it does not replace the RC2, still its best seller. PEW Science classes it as a higher-flow variant of SureFire "Total Signature Reduction". MSRP cited: RC2 $1,199, RC3 $1,799. — [TFB Silencer Saturday #307](https://cdn-fastly.thefirearmblog.com/blog/2023/12/09/surefire-socom-rc3/); [Guns & Ammo RC3 review](https://www.gunsandammo.com/editorial/surefire-socom556-rc3-full-review/517304); [PEW Science SSS.6.152 RC2 vs RC3](https://pewscience.com/sound-signature-reviews-free/sss-6-152-research-supplement-taming-the-mk18-rc2-vs-rc3)
- HUXWRX (formerly OSS) FLOW 556k: patented Flow-Through technology, marketed as reducing signature without changing cycling or service life; full-auto rated. Gun Digest notes that printing allowed HUXWRX to build low-back-pressure geometry it could not machine. — [Primary Arms listing](https://www.primaryarms.com/huxwrx-flow-556k-suppressor-only-black); [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)
- SIG SLX vs SLH (launched September 2021): SLX is optimised for supersonic use with emphasis on flash and toxic-fume reduction (claimed 70-80% fume reduction) and is Inconel; SLH is optimised for sound and for mixed supersonic/subsonic use (about 50% fume reduction) and includes titanium models. Both come in direct-thread or Clutch-LOK QD. — [SIG Sauer press release](https://www.sigsauer.com/blog/sig-sauer-introduces-the-next-generation-of-suppressor-technology-with-slx-and-slh-series-of-suppressors); [Soldier Systems Daily](https://soldiersystems.net/2021/09/17/sig-sauer-introduces-the-next-generation-of-suppressor-technology-with-slx-and-slh-series-of-suppressors/); [Gun Digest first look](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/first-look-sig-sauer-slx-and-slh-suppressor-series)
- SIG HEXIUM: newer SIG line, 3D printed and low back pressure; three titanium models plus one Inconel model rated for 5.56 NATO. — [TFB: New SIG HEXIUM suppressors](https://www.thefirearmblog.com/blog/new-sig-hexium-suppressors-3d-printed-low-back-pressure-44821573)
- B&T RBS ("Reduced Back pressure System") and SRBS (slimline version): B&T claims cyclic rate rises only about 4% versus up to 30%+ with traditional suppressors (manufacturer claim; no independent test found); rated for sustained automatic fire; ships with Rotex-SF HUB mount. — [B&T suppressor catalogue 2024](https://bt-ag.ch/wp-content/uploads/catalog/B&T_Catalogue_Suppressors_2024_EN.pdf); [Arms Unlimited SRBS listing](https://armsunlimited.com/bt-slimline-srbs-compact-reduced-back-pressure-quick-attach-223-556-rifle-suppressor-rotex-sf/)
- SilencerCo Velos LBP: SilencerCo's low-back-pressure 5.56 line (specs in section 7). — [SilencerCo 2024 catalogue](https://woodev.silencerco.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/CATALOG-2024.pdf)
- CGS Hyperion: described as having an annular perimeter cavity that works like a bore evacuator. — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)
- Dead Air has a "Nomad Flow" compared against other .308 cans by PEW Science. — [PEW Science SSS.6.115](https://pewscience.com/sound-signature-reviews-free/sss-6-115-research-supplement-nomad-flow-thunder-chicken-308-rifle-silencers)
- PEW Science maintains a dedicated low-back-pressure 5.56 comparison and a HUXWRX vs OCL vs SureFire "Taming the MK18" series. — [PEW Science SSS.6.169](https://pewscience.com/sound-signature-reviews-free/sss-6-169-research-supplement-low-back-pressure-556-silencers); [PEW Science SSS.6.84](https://pewscience.com/sound-signature-reviews-members-only/sss-6-84-research-supplement-taming-the-mk18)

### Inferences
- Game-stat framing: conventional = best muzzle sound reduction, worst "gas/blowback" (fouling, reliability drift, shooter exposure, raised rate of fire). Flow-through = minimal host penalty, slightly louder and flashier, weaker with subsonics. Hybrid/LBP (RC3, CAT, Hyperion, Velos LBP, RBS) = middle on both axes, usually at a price premium (RC3 costs 50% more than RC2).
- LBP matters most on short-barrelled gas-operated rifles and on belt-feds, and least on bolt actions (no action to over-gas), which is why precision cans remain conventional.
- The military value is not only noise: the SLX is sold on flash and toxic-fume reduction, i.e. shooter health and signature, not maximum quietness.

### Gaps
- Otter Creek Labs "Polonium Flow": search found no product by that name; only the standard Polonium/Polonium K. Could not confirm an OCL flow variant exists.
- CAT (Combat Application Technologies): beyond PEW's "Surge Bypass" classification, no model specs or independent reviews retrieved.
- No quantified, like-for-like dB or back-pressure numbers retrieved (PEW's detailed data is partly members-only).

## 4. Additive manufacturing (3D-printed titanium, Inconel, Haynes 282)

### Takeaway
Metal laser powder-bed printing (DMLS) is now mainstream in suppressors. It removes welds and assembly, makes all-Inconel bodies practical, and enables internal geometry that cannot be machined (flow channels, lattices/porous cores, finned blast chambers, annular bypass cavities). Costs: rougher surfaces, design limits on overhangs and powder removal, post-machining for threads, expensive machines.

### Cited Findings
- Process: suppressors designed in CAD and built by DMLS from powdered Inconel, titanium or stainless steel, fused layer by layer into one piece. — [Gun Digest: 3D printed suppressors](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor); [Silencer Shop blog](https://silencershop.com/blog/3d-printed-suppressor)
- Inconel is hard to machine, so traditionally it was used sparingly (often only the blast baffle); printing bypasses that and allows 100% Inconel silencers. — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)
- Limits: overhang/angle constraints, trapped powder must be able to escape, rougher surface finish, support removal, threads and mounts still machined, higher unit cost, printers cost $1M+. — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor); see also [Recoil: 3D printed suppressors, overhyped or the future?](https://www.recoilweb.com/3d-printed-suppressors-overhyped-or-technology-of-the-future-188854.html)
- Products and materials named:
  - HUXWRX FLOW 556k — DMLS 17-4 stainless (not titanium), flow-through internals. — [Primary Arms](https://www.primaryarms.com/huxwrx-flow-556k-suppressor-only-black); [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)
  - CGS Hyperion QD 762 — printed, annular bore-evacuator cavity. — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)
  - PTR Vent 1 — single-piece printed titanium with "Purposely Induced Porosity" (PIP): controlled porous structures and gas pathways in the core. — [PTR Industries: Vent 1](https://www.ptr-us.com/product/vent-1); [TFB Silencer Saturday #457](https://www.thefirearmblog.com/blog/silencer-saturday-457-ptr-vent-1-first-look-44831398)
  - SureFire RC3 — printed and machined internals inside a welded Inconel outer tube (hybrid construction, welded both ends as a fail-safe). — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)
  - Dead Air Lazarus 6 — printed Haynes 282. — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)
  - Radical Defense 556-GPS — Haynes 282, designed for belt-fed machine guns. — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)
  - Silencer Central Banish Speed K — printed; titanium version (light, not full-auto rated) and Inconel version (heavier, full-auto rated); finned blast chamber. Banish 9K — printed titanium, 2.7 oz, light enough to need no piston. — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor); [Silencer Central blog](https://www.silencercentral.com/blog/3d-printing-a-modern-method-to-suppressor-design/)
  - Primary Weapons Systems BDE — printed titanium with modular removable baffles. — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)
  - Delta P Design Brevis II — printed entirely in Inconel (belt-fed use) or titanium (bolt/semi-auto). — [Silencer Shop blog](https://silencershop.com/blog/3d-printed-suppressor)
  - SIG HEXIUM — printed, three titanium models and one Inconel 5.56 model. — [TFB](https://www.thefirearmblog.com/blog/new-sig-hexium-suppressors-3d-printed-low-back-pressure-44821573)
  - B&T Print-X RBS/SRBS — printed 718 Inconel. — [Silencer Shop: B&T Print-X SRBS 556 Compact](https://www.silencershop.com/b-t-srbs-556-compact.html); [Capitol Armory: B&T 762 Print-X RBS](https://capitolarmory.com/bt-srbs-762-full-size-steel-rifle-silencer.html)

### Inferences
- Printing and low back pressure are tightly coupled trends: most LBP cans are printed because coaxial flow paths are impractical to machine.
- Printing produces a recognisable look for art direction: seamless bodies, visible lattice or vented front caps, flared end caps (RC3), often raw grey or dark PVD finishes.
- The material choice on a printed can maps to duty rating: titanium = light, limited firing schedule; Inconel/Haynes 282 = heavy, machine-gun rated.

### Gaps
- SIG SLX/SLH being printed, and printed models from Q and KGM: commonly stated but not confirmed by any source I retrieved.
- SilencerCo Velos LBP construction (printed vs welded): not confirmed from sources retrieved.

## 5. Integral suppressors vs muzzle-mounted cans

### Takeaway
An integral suppressor is built around a (usually ported, often shortened) barrel as part of the weapon; a muzzle can is a detachable accessory. Integrals give the shortest overall quiet package and can make standard ammunition subsonic, but cost muzzle velocity, are tied to one gun, and are harder to maintain. The current trend is "optimised suppressed systems" (short barrel + dedicated can sold as a set) rather than classic ported integrals.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: integral suppressors are built into/around the barrel, with ports bleeding gas into surrounding chambers; maintenance requires at least partial disassembly of the firearm. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms))
- MP5SD: barrel ports reduce standard supersonic 9 mm to subsonic velocity; the benchmark integral SMG. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms)); [Breach Bang Clear](https://www.breachbangclear.com/?p=128292)
- De Lisle (.45 ACP, 85.5 dB), Welrod, HDM, AS Val / VSS (9x39): all integral designs (see section 1). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms))
- Ruger Silent-SR ISB: factory integrally suppressed barrel for the 10/22 Takedown; fits any 10/22 Takedown; about 113.2 dB claimed. Aftermarket equivalents: Gemtech MIST-22TD, Angstadt Arms Vanquish 22. — [Loadout Room: Ruger Silent-SR ISB](https://loadoutroom.com/silent-sr-isb-ruger-rolls-integrally-suppressed-barrel-1022-takedown/); [Recoil: Angstadt Vanquish 22](https://www.recoilweb.com/angstadt-arms-vanquish-22-x-fletcher-rifle-works-review-191493.html)
- SIG MCX lineage: the MCX grew out of a requirement to replace MP5SD-type roles; the Low Visibility Assault Weapon (LVAW, "Black Mamba") was a suppressed .300 Blackout MCX; the Rattler descends from the same line. SIG now sells an MCX-LTAW suppressor/SBR combo (6.75-inch barrel with factory-fitted suppressor) and the MCX Rattler Canebrake, built to be run suppressed. — [TFB Silencer Saturday #451: Return of the LVAW](https://www.thefirearmblog.com/blog/silencer-saturday-451-return-of-the-lvaw-44830645); [SIG: MCX-LTAW suppressor SBR combo](https://www.sigsauer.com/mcx-ltaw-supressor-sbr-combo.html); [SIG: MCX Canebrake](https://www.sigsauer.com/sig-mcx-canebrake.html)
- SIG MPX-SD: an integrally suppressed 9 mm MPX variant. — [Recoil](https://www.recoilweb.com/sig-mpx-optimized-mpxsd-integrally-suppressed-167460.html)

### Inferences
- Three sub-types worth separating in a game: (a) ported-barrel integrals (MP5SD, HDM, 10/22 ISB) — quiet with any ammo, lower damage/range; (b) dedicated-subsonic-cartridge integrals (AS Val/VSS, De Lisle, .300 BLK LVAW) — full performance of a heavy subsonic round, no velocity penalty by design; (c) "permanently attached or system-matched can" (SURG, MCX-LTAW, Rattler Canebrake) — a normal can sold as part of the weapon, handguard runs over it.
- Integrals should be non-removable attachments with no length penalty; muzzle cans add 4-9 inches and 7-20 oz but can move between weapons.

## 6. Mounting systems

### Takeaway
Three layers matter: the thread on the barrel, the interface on the can, and the adapter between them. The 1.375x24 "HUB" rear socket is now the de facto open standard that lets one can accept direct-thread, any brand's QD, three-lug or a pistol piston. SureFire SOCOM is the major closed ecosystem (and the military standard). Pistols with tilting barrels need a spring-loaded piston (Nielsen device); fixed-barrel guns do not.

### Cited Findings
- HUB: a 1.375x24 threaded socket in the rear of the suppressor body; also called "Universal", "Bravo" or 1-3/8x24. Adapters go outward from a HUB can to direct thread, KeyMo, Xeno, ASR, Plan B, three-lug or a piston; nothing can add HUB threads to a can built without them. — [TFB Silencer Saturday #253: The 1.375x24 universal mount explained](https://www.thefirearmblog.com/blog/2022/11/12/1-375-x-24-universal-mount-explained/); [Silencer Central: HUB mount, the new industry standard](https://www.silencercentral.com/blog/hub-mount-suppressors-the-new-industry-standard/); [Silencer Shop: HUB mount](https://silencershop.com/blog/suppressor-hub-mount)
- KeyMo (Dead Air): QD system locking onto Dead Air "Key" muzzle brakes/flash hiders; available as a HUB adapter with one-handed lock-up. Xeno (Dead Air): a taper-mount system, also offered as a HUB adapter. — [Silencer Central: Dead Air KeyMo HUB adapter](https://www.silencercentral.com/products/dead-air-keymo-hub-adapter); [Rifle Configurator: mounting systems 2026](https://www.rifleconfigurator.com/guides/suppressor-mounting-systems)
- ASR (SilencerCo "Active Spring Retention"): SilencerCo's QD system, offered as a factory HUB adapter. — [Rifle Configurator: suppressor mounts catalogue](https://www.rifleconfigurator.com/catalog/suppressor-mounts)
- Plan B (Q) / Rearden Atlas: taper-mount family; Rearden's Atlas adapter is listed as a HUB-to-Plan-B-compatible option. — [Rifle Configurator: mounting systems 2026](https://www.rifleconfigurator.com/guides/suppressor-mounting-systems)
- SureFire SOCOM: proprietary fast-attach interface on SureFire muzzle devices; SOCOM cans are not HUB and cannot be adapted to KeyMo, Xeno, ASR or Plan B. — [Rifle Configurator: mounting systems 2026](https://www.rifleconfigurator.com/guides/suppressor-mounting-systems)
- Other proprietary systems named: SIG Clutch-LOK (SLX/SLH), HUXWRX Torque Lock / QD 556, B&T Rotex (Rotex V for NATO flash hiders; Rotex-SF HUB assembly in titanium), YHM Phantom and sRx, TBAC SR mount. — [SIG press release](https://www.sigsauer.com/blog/sig-sauer-introduces-the-next-generation-of-suppressor-technology-with-slx-and-slh-series-of-suppressors); [Primary Arms: FLOW 556k](https://www.primaryarms.com/huxwrx-flow-556k-suppressor-only-black); [B&T catalogue 2024](https://bt-ag.ch/wp-content/uploads/catalog/B&T_Catalogue_Suppressors_2024_EN.pdf); [Silencer Central: HUB](https://www.silencercentral.com/blog/hub-mount-suppressors-the-new-industry-standard/); [Recoil: TBAC 338 Ultra SR](https://www.recoilweb.com/thunder-beast-releases-new-338-ultra-sr-silencer-144767.html)
- Three-lug: SMG/PCC quick-attach (MP5 heritage); available as HUB-pattern mounts from YHM and Rugged. — [Silencer Central: HUB](https://www.silencercentral.com/blog/hub-mount-suppressors-the-new-industry-standard/)
- Piston / Nielsen device / booster: a spring-loaded coupling between barrel and suppressor that momentarily decouples the can's mass so a tilting-barrel (Browning-type) pistol can unlock and cycle; needed if the barrel tilts or moves, not needed on fixed barrels. .45-size boosters also use 1.375x24 housings. — [SilencerCo: what is a Nielsen device](https://silencerco.com/blog/what-is-nielsen-device); [TFB Silencer Saturday #368: Breaking down Nielsen devices](https://www.thefirearmblog.com/blog/silencer-saturday-368-breaking-down-nielsen-devices-44819290); [Silencer Central](https://www.silencercentral.com/blog/what-is-a-nielsen-device/)
- Very light cans can skip the booster: the 2.7 oz printed-titanium Banish 9K needs no piston. — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)
- HUXWRX dealers warn that stainless (non-Inconel) flash hiders are not recommended with the FLOW 556k, i.e. muzzle device material matters under a can. — [Silencer Shop: HUXWRX Flow 556k](https://www.silencershop.com/huxwrx-flow-556k-l-in-black-magic.html)

### Inferences
- Game compatibility model: weapon has a muzzle interface (plain thread / brand muzzle device / three-lug); can has either a HUB socket (accepts any adapter item) or a locked proprietary mount (SureFire, Clutch-LOK). Direct thread = lightest, cheapest, slow to attach, can loosen; QD = fast swap, adds weight/length and cost; taper mounts = middle ground; three-lug = fastest, SMG only; piston = mandatory for tilting-barrel pistols, forbidden on fixed-barrel/rifle hosts.

### Gaps
- No fetched source directly comparing pros/cons of each QD brand (guns.com explainer blocked, HTTP 403). The pros/cons in Inferences are general knowledge, not cited.
- Thread standards by calibre (1/2x28 for 5.56/9 mm, 5/8x24 for .30, M13.5x1 LH, M14x1 LH for AK) appeared only incidentally in listings; not systematically sourced.

## 8. Military adoption trends

### Takeaway
Suppressors have moved from special-operations kit to general issue. The USMC began fielding the KAC NT4 across infantry 5.56 rifles in December 2020; the US Army's NGSW weapons (M7 rifle, M250 automatic rifle) are issued with SIG SLX suppressors as part of the system; SOCOM bought a purpose-built continuously suppressed upper (SURG). The stated rationale is command-and-control (hearing orders), reduced signature, and hearing protection rather than stealth.

### Cited Findings
- USMC: Marine Corps Systems Command began widespread fielding in December 2020 to infantry, reconnaissance and special operations units for M27, M4 and M4A1; target of about 30,000 suppressors by FY2023; described as the first service to issue suppressors for general use. [primary] — [MARCORSYSCOM: Marine Corps begins widespread fielding of suppressors](https://www.marcorsyscom.marines.mil/News/Article/2458429/marine-corps-begins-widespread-fielding-of-suppressors/); [Marine Corps Times](https://marinecorpstimes.com/news/your-marine-corps/2020/12/30/marine-corps-to-start-widespread-distribution-of-suppressors)
- Selection: RFI issued August 2017 for an off-the-shelf suppressor; Knight's Armament NT4 announced as selected in July 2020. — [TFB: USMC begins roll out of suppressors](https://cdn-fastly.thefirearmblog.com/blog/2021/01/13/usmc-suppressor-roll-out/)
- Background: USMC interest in suppressing every weapon dates to 2015-16, including a 2016 "Sea Dragon" experiment where nearly every weapon in an infantry battalion was suppressed; the Corps reported suppressors were highly beneficial and significantly improved command and control. — [TFB](https://cdn-fastly.thefirearmblog.com/blog/2021/01/13/usmc-suppressor-roll-out/); [SilencerCo: upcoming changes in military small arms](https://silencerco.com/blog/upcoming-changes-military-small-arms)
- Quantities conflict: "30,000 by FY2023" is the headline; one report gives a procurement figure of 25,853 5.56 mm suppressors; about 6,700 were delivered via an earlier logistics channel and about 7,000 more bought in 2020; an early industry call mentioned up to 194,000 units to cover every Marine's 5.56 weapon. — [guns.com: Marines expanding suppressor use to field 30,000](https://www.guns.com/news/2021/01/05/marines-expanding-suppressor-use-to-field-30-000); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms)); [National Interest](https://nationalinterest.org/node/176099)
- US Army NGSW: SIG Sauer won a 10-year contract in 2022 for the XM7 rifle and XM250 light machine gun (replacing M4 and M249 in close-combat units); the rifle was type-classified as the M7 in May 2025; it weighs 8.38 lb bare and 9.84 lb with its suppressor (i.e. the can adds about 1.46 lb). The contract includes options for USMC and USSOCOM; the USMC has said it is tracking but has not announced adoption. — [The War Zone: M7 gets Army's seal of approval](https://www.twz.com/land/sig-sauers-m7-rifle-gets-official-army-seal-of-approval-despite-controversy); [Wikipedia: XM7 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XM7_rifle)
- An improved M7 configuration has been shown with a shorter suppressor and lightweight thermal shield that delays visible heat signature under night-vision/thermal until about 100 rounds versus about 40 with the earlier suppressor; wider issue/retrofit not confirmed at the time of the source. — [The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/land/sig-sauers-m7-rifle-gets-official-army-seal-of-approval-despite-controversy)
- The M7 programme has drawn public criticism (an Army captain's paper calling it unfit; SIG disputes this). — [The War Zone: Army captain slams XM7](https://www.twz.com/land/army-captain-slams-new-xm7-rifle-as-unfit-sig-sauer-says-otherwise)
- USSOCOM SURG: August 2018 five-year IDIQ award to SIG Sauer worth about $48 million (one outlet $49M), expected completion July 2023; package is a complete MCX upper for M4A1 lowers with direct-thread Inconel suppressor, removable titanium cage, SD and low-profile handguards, folding stock kit, 5.56 NATO; goal was a weapon that can be fired continuously suppressed with better thermal behaviour and less blowback than adding a can to a standard upper. A later sole-source notice extended it two years (value/date not found). — [Military Times / GearScout](https://www.militarytimes.com/off-duty/gearscout/2018/08/13/socom-is-buying-this-new-suppressed-upper-receiver-group-to-make-its-troops-quieter-and-deadlier/); [Soldier Systems Daily first look](https://soldiersystems.net/2018/08/02/first-look-socoms-new-suppressed-upper-receiver-group-from-sig-sauer/); [Military.com](https://www.military.com/kitup/2018/08/08/sig-gets-48-million-pentagon-contract-continuous-rifle-suppression.html)

### Inferences
- Military requirements now prioritise (in roughly this order) reliability/low back pressure, flash and thermal signature, toxic gas exposure, durability on automatic fire, and only then absolute sound reduction. That explains Inconel SLX and RBS-type cans and thermal shields/cages as standard accessories.
- For the game: a general-issue "duty" can should be modelled as heavy, very durable, modest sound reduction, strong flash reduction, with heat mirage/thermal glow as its main drawback; suppressor covers/heat shields are a legitimate sub-attachment.

### Gaps
- No post-2021 confirmation of whether the USMC met its 30,000 target, and no NT4 specs retrieved.
- No primary Army document retrieved naming the exact SLX model on M7/M250 or its specs; the 1.46 lb figure is derived from the two weights in secondary sources.
- Non-US adoption (e.g. UK, Nordic, Ukrainian/Russian wartime practice) not researched.

## 9. Materials and coatings

### Takeaway
Material is the main lever on weight versus firing schedule. Aluminium: lightest, rimfire/pistol only. Titanium: light and strong, standard for hunting/precision and light rifle cans, limited under sustained automatic fire. 17-4 stainless: the workhorse, heavier, durable, affordable. Inconel 718 / Stellite / Haynes 282: heaviest and most heat- and erosion-resistant, used for blast baffles or entire machine-gun-rated cans.

### Cited Findings
- Bodies are typically steel, aluminium or titanium; baffles stainless, aluminium, titanium or Inconel; aluminium wears quickly under full-auto fire. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silencer_(firearms))
- Titanium is valued for light weight, Inconel for durability; Inconel was traditionally confined to the blast baffle because it is hard to machine; printing enables all-Inconel cans. — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)
- Duty-rating pairs that show the trade directly: Banish Speed K titanium (light, not full-auto rated) vs Inconel (heavier, full-auto rated); Delta P Brevis II Inconel for belt-feds vs titanium for bolt/semi-auto. — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor); [Silencer Shop blog](https://silencershop.com/blog/3d-printed-suppressor)
- Haynes 282 superalloy: used in Dead Air Lazarus 6 and Radical Defense 556-GPS (belt-fed rated). — [Gun Digest](https://gundigest.com/gear-ammo/suppressors/3d-printed-suppressor)

### Inferences
- Simple game mapping: aluminium = weight x0.5, rimfire/pistol only, low durability; titanium = weight x0.6-0.7, semi-auto/bolt, overheats on sustained fire; 17-4 stainless = baseline weight, full-auto capable on moderate schedules; Inconel/Stellite/Haynes 282 = weight x1.2-1.3, belt-fed rated, most expensive.
- For the same alloy family, the titanium premium buys roughly 30-45% weight saving at .30 calibre (9-12 oz versus 14-18 oz in the examples above).

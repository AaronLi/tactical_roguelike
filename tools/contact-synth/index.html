Contact Synth

Metal & plastic contact sounds from a physical description: gesture, bodies and springiness.

How sounds are described

Every sound is the same recipe, so any combination of the sliders is a valid sound:

  1. Gesture: how the two bodies touch over time. An impact is a momentum kick with bounces. Friction reads a surface texture (roughness, grain, periodicity) at the sliding speed, plus stick-slip for squeaks. A snap loads (creaks), releases (click) and lands on its stop (seat). Use any or all of them at once.
  2. Contact: Hertzian contact time from both materials' stiffness, the mass and the curvature. Metal on metal is a ~30 µs spike (bright), plastic ~300 µs (duller), rubber milliseconds (a thud).
  3. Bodies: each is a material (stiffness, density, internal loss) plus a geometry (shape, size, thickness) and how it's held. This gives its modes: pitch ∝ thickness / size², ring time ∝ 1 / (loss × frequency).
  4. Springiness: a flex swing (rulers, sheets, latch arms) that can rattle or bend the pitch, and a coil waveguide whose dispersion turns every click into a "zing" that echoes along the spring.
